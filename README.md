# 🏙️ Chicago Crime Dataset — Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

A comprehensive, evidence-based Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) of the Chicago Crime dataset using Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn, and SciPy.

---

## 📌 Project Overview & Objective

The objective of this project is to perform an end-to-end data science exploratory analysis on Chicago crime records to uncover structural patterns, assess data quality, evaluate temporal and geographic trends, analyze arrest rates across crime categories, and test statistical hypotheses.

Every finding, statistic, and visualization in this repository is derived directly from the underlying dataset without fabrication or unsupported causal claims.

## 🚀 Key Findings

1. **Dominant Crime Types:** Theft (19.6%), Battery (18.5%), Criminal Damage (9.4%), Assault (8.6%), and Other Offense (6.8%) constitute over 60% of all reported incidents in Chicago.
2. **Overall Arrest Rate:** The baseline arrest rate is **32.8%** across all recorded crimes.
3. **Arrest Rate Disparity:** Arrest probabilities vary dramatically by crime type — ranging from **8.1%** (Deceptive Practice) and **8.9%** (Motor Vehicle Theft) to **84.3%** (Weapons Violation) and **98.9%** (Narcotics).
4. **Temporal Patterns:**
   - **Hourly:** Crime incidents peak around midnight (12:00 AM) and reach their minimum at 5:00 AM.
   - **Monthly:** Crime shows distinct seasonal variation, peaking in summer months (July/August) and dropping in winter (February).
   - **Day of Week:** Friday experiences the highest incident volume (17,807), while Tuesday records the lowest (16,870).
5. **Domestic Crimes:** **19.3%** of all reported crime incidents are classified as domestic.
6. **Geographic Concentration:** Over 27.8% of crimes occur on streets/sidewalks. Police Districts 11, 8, 6, and 1 record the highest incident counts.
7. **Statistical Significance:** Chi-square tests confirm a highly significant relationship between crime type and arrest outcome ($\chi^2 = 33,377.64, p < 0.001$), with a strong effect size ($\text{Cramér's } V = 0.553$).

## 🛠️ Data Pipeline & Methodology

1. **Data Ingestion & Identification:** Auto-detects data source, maps standard Chicago Police Department column formats.
2. **Data Overview & Quality Audit:** Computes memory footprint, data types, missing value percentages, duplicate counts, string whitespace consistency, and geographic spatial bounding checks (Chicago coordinates: Lat 41.6–42.1, Lon -87.95 to -87.5).
3. **Temporal Feature Extraction:** Parses datetime values to extract `Year`, `Month`, `Month_Name`, `Day`, `Day_of_Week`, `Hour`, `Quarter`, and `Is_Weekend`.
4. **Univariate Analysis:** Analyzes categorical frequencies (Primary Type, Description, Location Description) and target distributions (Arrest, Domestic).
5. **Bivariate Analysis:** Computes arrest rates by crime category, tracks crime trends across years and months, and examines crime distribution across hours and days of the week.
6. **Multivariate Analysis:** Evaluates 2D/3D interaction spaces including Day of Week × Hour Heatmaps, Crime Type × Hour × Arrest Rate, and District × Crime Type distribution matrices.
7. **Geographic Analysis:** Constructs spatial scatter plots using transparency (`alpha`) across 118,882 valid geocoded incidents and multi-panel maps for top crime categories.
8. **Correlation & Statistical Testing:** Calculates Pearson correlation matrices for numerical metrics and performs Chi-Square tests of independence with Cramér's V effect size estimates.

## ⚠️ Limitations & Analytical Caveats

- **Reported Crime Bias:** Dataset reflects *reported* incidents handled by police departments; unreported crimes are absent.
- **Correlation vs. Causation:** Statistical associations (e.g., seasonal crime peaks in summer) demonstrate co-occurrence, not direct causality.
- **Enforcement Disparities:** High arrest rates in categories like Narcotics (98.9%) or Weapons Violations (84.3%) reflect proactive enforcement strategies where arrest is inherent to incident discovery, rather than higher clearance of reactive complaints.
- **Geographic Imprecision:** Small percentages of records lack valid geocoding coordinates and are excluded from spatial mapping.
